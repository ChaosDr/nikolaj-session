AN IMMERSIVE CASE STUDY EXERCISE

The First Session: Nikolaj

You are the therapist. A new client, twenty-four, has just sat down across from you. Read the room, choose how to respond, and pay close attention to how he reacts.

Two gauges track him: Shame, how threatened he feels, and Trust, how safe he feels with you. Push too hard before trust exists and he may walk out. Play it too safe and the hour changes nothing. There is no perfect script.

Reading your trace

  • Where did shame climb fastest, and what in Nikolaj's story made that a sore spot?
  • Which choices felt kind but moved nothing? What might it cost him to be asked more?
  • If a question was right but too early, what would you need to see in him to know it is time?

Take it to your group

Before anything else: choose one detail from Nikolaj's story that you find important or puzzling, and invite each person in your group to share theirs.

Your task: build an interpretation of what keeps Nikolaj stuck and explore what might help. Several interpretations may fit, so use details from the case to explain yours. Start with a question that interests your group. You don't need to finish every question or agree with each other.

When you have an initial answer, stay with it a little longer:

  • What in the story makes us think that?
  • What else could explain the same behavior?
  • What would we need to know to tell the difference?

For example, “he blames everyone else” is a starting point. But what might he be protecting himself from with that? Shame, fear of being unwanted, the loss of his father's approval, or something else? Would those possibilities call for the same response?

Changing your mind, questioning an interpretation, and noticing missing information all count as contributions. You don't need to share personal experiences. Please discuss without AI. No written answers are required.
